## In detail: the three answers and how they are decided

Live from the semantic layer (`usl demo` step 11, `docs/zetaris-demo.md`):

| Read from | Node-positive |
|---|---|
| the registry's N category column | **336** |
| the registry's positive-node count column | **328** |
| the quote-checked reading (the verdict column, `stage_check.fault_field`): where the two columns disagree, the field the pathology report contradicts is set aside; where they agree, they stand | **333**, and 2 left to a person |

The two columns disagree on 10 of 663 records where both apply, and the basic source-level check (positive nodes never exceed nodes examined) passes on all 1,097: the errors are visible only across fields. One of the 10, TCGA-E2-A572, is recorded with 0 positive nodes; its report says "Lymph node status: Positive 8 / 13". The 333 is the quote-backed reading, not ground truth: the report decides only the 10 disagreements (8 settled, 2 to a person), and those readings were scored against blind labels from one lay labeller (the builder): 8 of the 10 agree; on TCGA-BH-A0BS and TCGA-E2-A1LI the label leaves the decision to a person, and the Ultra second reader also objects to E2-A1LI's reading (`docs/evidence/baseline-m0-results.md`, `docs/evidence/ultra-critic-results.md`). The stored values were first written by earlier NVIDIA-direct reads without receipts; on those 10 records the latest evidence runs (20 steps, each with a Nightshift receipt whose signature was checked at run time against a pinned key) reproduce all 18 stored report values (each with its receipt: `docs/evidence/nightshift-provenance.md`).

**How it decides**
1. **The semantic layer proves the record is impossible.** A SQL rule in the Zetaris USL, over three live sources (the NCI GDC API, a file store, Postgres): the recorded N category and the record's own node count cannot both be true (24 of 1,097, plus 1 missing N the count could fill).
2. **Nemotron may only quote the report.** Lightning reads first with thinking off; Super VL with a 4k thinking budget reads what is still open; Nemotron Parse reads the original scan. Every text call the product makes goes through Nightshift with a signed receipt; Parse goes to NVIDIA direct.
3. **Code checks every quote:** it must be in the report and show the value; otherwise the value is rejected with a reason.
4. **The semantic layer names the field** the report contradicts (node count, N category, or a person decides), and **a person decides**. Every write is append-only.

**Measured, pre-registered where marked** (each number links to its file):
- The verdict names a field for **21 of the 25** queue records (24 impossible + 1 missing N; 8 node count, 13 N category); 4 go to a person ([Results](#results-live-2026-10-22-to-10-24-every-number-has-its-file)).
- **Red team (pre-registered):** Nemotron as a Markovian attacker, 0 wrong accepts in 57 judged attacks ([v3](docs/evidence/adversary-chain-v3-results.md)).
- **Reasoning dial (pre-registered):** thinking off first, then 4k, used 40% fewer tokens than always 4k (measured with Super at the first read; the shipped chain, with Lightning there, was not measured for cost); 0 of 31 cheap closings contradicted ([dial](docs/evidence/dial-sample-results.md)).
- **Model per step (pre-registered):** Lightning took the first read from Super (18 vs 15 of 28 settled; of 38 closings 33 confirmed, 0 contradicted, 5 unconfirmed; a controlled same-session re-run confirmed it settles more (16–17 vs 13–14 of 28 in each of 3 trials, 0 contradicted) but showed it is **not faster** (median 2.17 s vs 1.92 s per read) and uses more tokens, [timing](docs/evidence/r1-timing-results.md), [Lightning](docs/evidence/lightning-r1-results.md)); Ultra lost the case-note seat to Super (8 vs 21 of 25, [Ultra](docs/evidence/ultra-notes-results.md)).
- **An agent reading the layer (pre-registered, 3 probes, 30 calls):** given only the descriptions, Super wrote a query in 19 of 20 calls and all 19 read the raw count column: 14 returned 476, leaving out the 6 patients the verdict corrects; 3 returned 0 because the descriptions did not say counts are stored as "4.0"; 1 was refused by our read guard; the last one, the only query that also read the checked column, included them but filled the 3 cases left to a person back in from the raw count (482, not 479). A pointer to the verdict changed nothing (0 of 5 with it, 0 of 5 without), and neither did the layer's own data-quality rules rendered into the schema as `LOAD USL` returns them: all 10 queries of the third probe, with and without the rules, counted the raw column and returned 476 ([probe 1](docs/evidence/agent-probe-results.md), [probe 2](docs/evidence/agent-probe-2-results.md), [probe 3](docs/evidence/agent-probe-3-results.md)). So the product writes the verdict into the layer and answers the question itself.
- **Nemotron alone vs the product, against blind labels from one lay labeller, the builder (pre-registered):** the product agreed with the label on **18 of 25** cases, Super alone with 4k thinking on 17, Super with thinking off on 9, the count rule alone on 7. On accuracy the product and Super-with-thinking are within one case; what the product adds is a guard-checked quote and an append-only record for every value (Super alone also ran through Nightshift with signed receipts) ([baseline](docs/evidence/baseline-m0-results.md); one lay labeller, a standard close to the product's own logic, both disclosed before scoring).

## Why it matters: agents trust the semantic layer

Agents now read data through semantic layers: the layer tells them what every table and column means, so they can query without ETL. But a semantic layer gives meaning, not truth. The layer describes TCGA-E2-A572's two columns perfectly, and anything counting node-positive patients from the count column leaves this patient out, with nothing in the chain noticing. Proof of Stage puts the missing piece inside the layer itself: a verdict per record (`stage_check.fault_field`) naming which field the source document contradicts, next to the accepted reading and its verbatim quote (`derived_stage`, also in the layer). Anything that reads `stage_check` gets that verdict next to the record, and the layer's own descriptions of the N and count columns (what an agent reads to learn the schema) point to it (`040_descriptions.sql`). We tested whether that is enough (pre-registered, `docs/evidence/agent-probe-results.md`): Nemotron Super, through Nightshift, given only the layer's table and column descriptions and asked "how many patients are node-positive?", wrote a working query 9 of 10 times and used `fault_field` **0 of 10 times**, with or without the pointers. Its answer counted from the count column and left out all 6 patients the report shows node-positive but the count records as 0, E2-A572 among them. A pointer in the description was not enough. A follow-up (pre-registered, `docs/evidence/agent-probe-2-results.md`) put the checked count itself into the layer, beside the raw one (`stage_check.positive_nodes_checked`): the agent read it in 1 of 5 calls; that query did include the 6 patients, but filled the 3 cases left to a person back in from the raw count (482, not 479). So we do not claim agents pick up the verdict on their own: it is for the people and tools that read `stage_check` and the case page. This matches a recent controlled study of agents and data-quality metadata (Besanson, "SARC-DQ: Runtime Data-Quality Gating for Agentic AI", arXiv:2607.26313, 2026): competent agents silently acted on defects carried in metadata about 60% of the time while raising no flags, and a check placed before the action recovered the loss on the signals it covered. In Proof of Stage that check is the person deciding. A design lesson from that one query (not re-tested): the checked count leaves every case for a person empty, the agent read empty as missing data and filled it from the raw count; even counted directly, the checked count gives 331 on the first-screen cohort where the verdict gives 333, because two cases left to a person are node-positive on both registry columns (`usl demo` step 11). Of the 10 records where the columns disagree, the quote-verified report reading settles 8 (6 node-positive patients recorded with 0 positive nodes, 2 node-negative patients recorded N1) and leaves 2 to a person (`usl demo` step 12); 14 more contradictions keep the same yes/no answer but change the N category. `nodes_within_tested` holds on all 1,097 records; only the cross-field rule sees the 24. Scored against blind human labels (one lay labeller), the layer agrees on 8 of those 10; on the other 2 the label leaves the case to a person.

The problem is not medical: a database says X, the source document says Y. Which field is wrong, and can you prove it? Cancer staging on NCI's public data is the worked, measured example; you do not need to know staging to follow it, only that the count and the category disagree and the report settles it.

**Pointing it at other data.** Reused as-is: the record / source-document / rule / verified-reading / verdict pattern, append-only writes, signed receipts, the evidence chain, the red-team chain, the case page. Rebuilt per domain: the consistency rules (SQL in the layer) and the guard's field checkers (ours knows how pathology reports write node counts; dates, amounts and identifiers are simpler). Candidate uses, untested: clinical-trial source data verification (the trial database against patients' source documents, today done by hand), registry and research-dataset audits, claims and forms against their attached documents. Built and measured on breast cancer staging; four pre-registered tries on other data are in [Pointing it at other data](#pointing-it-at-other-data).
